Historical Context:

For centuries, pre-modern Japanese society openly celebrated Nanshoku, the traditional love and intimacy between adult men and young companions, which was deeply embedded among samurai warriors and Buddhist monks. This indigenous acceptance was abruptly attacked during the Meiji Restoration when intense pressure to adopt Western legal standards led to a brief criminalization of sodomy between 1872 and 1882. Although the state quickly dropped those foreign laws, the modern conservative ruling party in Tokyo displays immense historical amnesia by blocking formal marriage equality. Politicians claim that recognizing gay relationships destroys national tradition, a ridiculous stance that willfully ignores centuries of their own celebrated samurai culture.

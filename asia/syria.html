Historical Context:

The ancient cosmopolitan cities of Damascus and Aleppo historically served as vibrant hubs where poetry and social customs accommodated same-sex male affection long before modern borders were carved. This native acceptance was crushed in 1949 when French Mandate legal structures were formalized into Article 520, criminalizing gay relations with prison time. Today, the country is a fragmented landscape of extreme violence and profound political shifts. The Assad regime had weaponized the old French law to arrest gay men to boost its moral authority, but that did not save it from collapsing in December 2024 when a massive rebel alliance seized Damascus and forced the dictator to flee. While ISIS continues to run shadow operations from the deserts, a new transitional government led by conservative Islamist factions now controls the capital, enforcing its own severe crackdowns and keeping the territory highly dangerous for gay men.

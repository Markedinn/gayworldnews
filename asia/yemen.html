This legal evidence documents a period from around 800 BC until 600 AD. Laws show that tribes prioritize bloodlines and alliances over personal attachments. Stories are passed around by word of mouth, so we cannot be sure about the culture, but we are certain that the state does not prosecute gay men.

The Rasulid dynasty rules southern Yemen from the capital in Taiz. The Rasulids run a lavish, wealthy, and highly secular court, employing court poets to write homoerotic ghazal verses celebrating male beauty.

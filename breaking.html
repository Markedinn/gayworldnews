SENEGAL (Legislative Update)

National Assembly Passes Bill Doubling Prison Sentences to 10 Years
‘Homosexuals will no longer breathe in this country’

(March 12, 2026)

In a devastating blow to human rights, Senegal’s National Assembly has passed a bill that significantly increases the penalties for consensual same-sex acts. The new legislation doubles the maximum prison term from five to 10 years for those convicted under the country’s "acts against nature" laws. This move, backed by Prime Minister Ousmane Sonko, specifically targets the LGBTQ+ community and any allies providing advocacy or support.

Beyond imprisonment, the law introduces heavy fines and criminalizes the "promotion" of LGBTQ+ identities, effectively banning advocacy groups. While the bill awaits the final signature of the President, local defenders report that the public debate has already triggered a wave of "outings" and vigilante violence in Dakar. As the climate becomes increasingly hostile, many in the community are urgently seeking pathways to safety.

Source: Associated Press / Reuters

UNITED KINGDOM (Healthcare Update)

NHS Immediately Pauses All Hormone Treatments for Youth Under 18

(March 10, 2026)

Health Secretary Wes Streeting has officially confirmed an immediate pause on all prescriptions of puberty blockers and hormones for transgender youth under the age of 18. This decision follows a review suggesting a lack of long-term evidence regarding the clinical safety and effectiveness of such treatments for minors. This move shifts the UK toward a more restrictive model of care, aligning with recent changes seen in several other European nations.

While the government maintains that the pause is a necessary safety precaution, advocacy groups have labeled the decision a "grave betrayal" that will exacerbate mental health crises among vulnerable youth. As the UK's first openly gay Health Secretary, Streeting faces significant pressure to reconsider the ban. A public consultation is currently underway to determine the permanent future of these services, but for now, no new referrals will be accepted for hormone therapy.

Source: UK Parliament / NHS England

CAMEROON (Prison Release)

Five Men Released from Kondengui Prison Following 4-Month Sentence

(March 6, 2026)

Five men have been released from Kondengui Central Prison in Yaoundé after being held for four months on homosexuality charges. The group, including four Cameroonians and one Congolese national, were arrested following a targeted police operation involving illegal phone searches. Their release marks a rare legal victory in a country that frequently uses Article 347-1 of the Penal Code to prosecute individuals based on perceived sexual orientation.

The advocacy group SOS Solidarity spearheaded the efforts for their release, highlighting that the arrests were based on entrapment. While the men are now physically free, they face severe social consequences, including loss of employment and family ostracization. Activists note that this case underscores the ongoing danger for LGBTQ+ people in Cameroon, where the legal system is often weaponized for extortion and arbitrary detention.

Source: Erasing 76 Crimes / SOS Solidarity
